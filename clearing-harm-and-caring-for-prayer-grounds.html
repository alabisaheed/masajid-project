Clearing Harm and Keeping Mosque Grounds Safe as Ongoing Charity

AS
Alabi Saheed
15 Aug 2026 • 4 min read

During the time of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), there was a humble woman named Umm Mihjan who made it her regular habit to sweep and clean the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah.

She was not wealthy or famous, but she loved taking care of the house of Allah. When she passed away, the companions buried her quietly. The next day, the Prophet (peace be upon him) noticed her absence and asked about her. When told she had passed away, he asked to be taken to her grave and prayed the funeral prayer over her, showing how deeply Allah and His Messenger valued her simple service.

"Removing a harmful thing from the pathway is a charity." — Sahih al-Bukhari & Muslim

Protecting Worshippers in Rural Mosques

In many developing communities, uncompleted mosques and open prayer shelters become overgrown with dense weeds during the rainy season. This overgrowth attracts dangerous snakes, scorpions, and pests that pose a real danger to worshippers arriving in the dark for early dawn (Fajr) or night (Isha) prayers.

Funding chemical weed clearing, trimming overgrowth, and maintaining clean paths removes danger from the path of believers. It ensures that mothers, children, and elderly worshippers can walk to the mosque in safety and tranquility.
